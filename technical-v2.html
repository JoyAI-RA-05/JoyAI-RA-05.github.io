Overview

Abstract

Robot data is scarce, so generalist policies need to learn from heterogeneous sources, including human egocentric video, simulation, and real robots, which differ in supervision and embodiment, with action labels missing or mutually incompatible. Human egocentric data scale best but sit farthest from robot data, and naive pooling causes negative transfer rather than knowledge sharing. We present JoyAI-RA 0.5, a generalist Vision-Language-World-Action (VLWA) framework that couples physical world-dynamics priors with visual semantics and scales manipulation learning across such data via dual action alignment. Implicit action alignment infers latent actions from visual transitions, enabling action-free human, simulation, and robot data to guide a latent-action-conditioned world model in learning physical dynamics. Explicit alignment grounds reliable human and robot trajectories in a unified physical action space through a canonical action representation and camera-frame chunk-relative end-effector actions. An inner-outer-loop reinforcement stage then pairs efficient task adaptation with foundation-policy improvement. On a real-world AgiBot benchmark, JoyAI-RA performs strongly on both seen tasks and unseen variations. The task score improves consistently as the volume of human egocentric pretraining data increases and shows no sign of saturation at our largest scale. This suggests that abundant but weakly labeled human experience can be converted into a transferable training signal, making human video not merely a weak auxiliary source but a primary axis along which manipulation capability can be scaled.

Experiments

Real-World AgiBot Benchmark

We evaluate six real-world AgiBot G1 scenarios under seen tasks and unseen variations in objects, spatial layouts, backgrounds, and illumination, reporting subtask-completion scores over 20 seen and 10 unseen trials per task.

Human Video Scaling

Human video is not auxiliary; it is a scaling axis. Across controlled studies, more egocentric video expands action diversity, improves learned dynamics, and yields stronger policy initialization with no observed saturation at the largest scale. As the EgoLive corpus grows, its action-centric representations cover broader and lower-density regions of the embedding space.

LAC-WM pretraining

Scaling the full human-video corpus from 10% to 100% improves seen score from 83.13 to 97.50 and unseen score from 56.88 to 72.40, with robot data and downstream policy training held fixed.

Policy pretraining

Scaling transfers to robot control: seen score improves from 47.8 to 85.6 and unseen score improves from 37.6 to 60.2, with identical robot-action post-training.

Inner–Outer Loop Reinforcement Learning

The inner loop rapidly learns a lightweight residual policy for task-level adaptation, while the asynchronous outer loop incorporates accumulated interaction data into the foundation VLWA and periodically sends an improved model back to the edge.

Under unseen object-position shifts, both loops improve over the original policy, and their combination reaches a 70% success rate on Mouse and 50% on Headphone pick-and-place tasks.
